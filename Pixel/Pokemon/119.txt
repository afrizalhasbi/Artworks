Next on my list is Seaking, which appears to be an easy draw at first glance. And after my prior experiences in this marathon, it does feel easier. For starters, I begin again with the sketch-blob method, but I paid more attention to the shape. What I learnt from Krabby is that even if I do use this sketch-blob rough draft, I still need to do manual interventions on individual pixels on the outline to make the shape more legible and cohesive.

I think this turn out decently, though still kind of awkward looking. But there's one thing I learned from this:

Flat, cloth-like shape. For a lack of better term, let's call these cloths. Seaking has this many fins on its form which are flat and cloth-like. It can fold, it can bend, and these folding and bending should be apparent in the inner details and the shading. I call these cloth because I have this trouble, too, trying to sketch out clothes-folding in the past on other exercises. I don't think I fully understand how to make good folds yet. As I see it the original reference have a more pronounced, indeed, more cohesive outward curves. I should aspire to improve my lineart, once again.

Proportions. Trying to sprinkle the details on the body and fins, I find myself ended up lacking in space, and I could not put all te details on the same location. Perhaps this is not a big deal, though, as I am just making a reference copy. But it does look weird that the horn sticks out way too much in my version (I only notcied this when zooming out).
